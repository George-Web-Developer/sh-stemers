04 APOLLO 11 · THE FINAL APPROACH

Eagle's Powered Descent.

The final powered descent of Apollo 11 was a sequence of high-precision guidance and manual crew decision-making under critical propellant margins.

  • 1202 & 1201 Apollo Guidance Computer Alarms Executive overflow condition: the rendezvous radar was in the wrong mode, feeding redundant cycles into the Apollo Guidance Computer. Steve Bales in Mission Control gave the famous "GO" call as core descent guidance tasks remained unaffected.
  • Low-Propellant Quantity Warning (Bingo Call) With under 90 seconds of fuel remaining before the mandatory abort threshold, Neil Armstrong leveled off the lander to fly over a boulder-strewn crater (West Crater) to find a smooth, safe touchdown site.
  • Manual Attitude Takeover Armstrong switched the flight controller to semi-manual mode (P66) to counteract horizontal drift and ease Eagle gently onto the regolith with less than 45 seconds of fuel remaining.

Journal reference: NASA History — Apollo Lunar Surface Journal.

06 FLIGHT PHYSICS & REPLAY VALIDATION

Orbital Descent Mechanics.

Mathematical flight mechanics integrating variable-mass Tsiolkovsky rocket dynamics, pitch/roll reaction control torque, and local lunar gravitational acceleration.

Braking Phase

Kill high orbital velocity (~1,700 m/s) at high altitude. Manage continuous main engine thrust to bring down descent rate before closing stopping distance.

Pitch-Over & Approach

Rotate lander upright to establish direct radar ground lock and visually locate candidate landing site. Damp lateral drift velocities.

Final Terminal Descent

Switch to fine throttle adjustments. Regulate vertical speed below 2.5 m/s and horizontal drift under 1.5 m/s while maintaining attitude within 10°.

Touchdown & Engine Cutoff

Deployable 1.5-meter contact probes touch regolith first, illuminating CONTACT lamps on the instrument panel. Pilot commands immediate main engine shutdown.

Tsiolkovsky Variable-Mass Acceleration

As propellant is consumed through the high-expansion rocket bell, vehicle mass decreases, causing available thrust-to-weight acceleration to continuously increase.

JDK Server-Side Replay Verification

Browser flight telemetry is recorded in a fixed-step input vector and replayed deterministically by the Java HTTP server before admitting verified scores to the flight log.

// Deterministic Tsiolkovsky Flight Dynamics:
a_thrust = (throttle / 100) * maxThrust / totalMass
dm/dt    = -thrust / exhaustVelocity
a_net    = a_thrust * cos(pitch) * cos(roll) - g_lunar
v_vert   = v_vert + a_net * dt
y_pos    = y_pos + v_vert * dt
// Lunar Ephemeris & Horizon Geometry (Sun & Earth):
sin(el_sun)   = sin(lat) * sin(dec_sun) + cos(lat) * cos(dec_sun) * cos(H_sun)
tan(az_sun)   = sin(H_sun) / (cos(lat) * tan(dec_sun) - sin(lat) * cos(H_sun))
sin(el_earth) = sin(lat) * sin(dec_earth) + cos(lat) * cos(dec_earth) * cos(H_earth)
power_yield   = el_sun > mask ? 100 * cos(el_sun) : 0% (in shadow)
dte_link      = el_earth > mask ? "CONNECTED (+14 dB)" : "OCCLUDED (RELAY REQUIRED)"
